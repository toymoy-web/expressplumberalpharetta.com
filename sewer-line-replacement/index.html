When Replacement Is Necessary

Repair Vs. Replacement

Damage Along Most Of The Line

Multiple failure points found by camera usually means replacement is more cost-effective than repeated spot repairs.

Collapsed Or Severely Offset Pipe

A line that's structurally failed, not just cracked, needs full replacement.

Repeated Repairs Not Holding

If spot repairs keep failing in the same general area, the surrounding pipe is likely also compromised.

Original Clay Or Orangeburg Pipe

Very old pipe materials in some of Alpharetta's original-era homes are often past the point where spot repair makes sense.

Local Factors

What This Looks Like In Alpharetta

Some of Alpharetta's oldest homes near the historic core may still have original clay or, rarely, Orangeburg pipe from decades ago, both of which are common candidates for full replacement rather than repair.

Alpharetta's red clay soil and mature tree roots put ongoing stress on buried lines over decades, which is part of why older sections of pipe eventually reach the point where replacement is the more sensible option.

Trenchless methods have become more common in Alpharetta in recent years specifically because they avoid tearing up mature landscaping and hardscaping that's expensive to restore.

A full replacement is a bigger decision than a repair, so we walk through both trenchless and traditional options with real tradeoffs rather than defaulting to whichever is easier for us to schedule.

FAQs

Common Questions

How is this different from Sewer Line Repair?

Repair addresses a specific damaged section. Replacement is for a line that's failed extensively or has original pipe material past the point spot repair can reasonably fix. See our Sewer Line Repair page for the more limited scope.

Will trenchless replacement work for my yard?

It depends on the line's condition and path. We can tell you after the camera inspection whether trenchless is a realistic option for your property.

How long does a full replacement take?

Traditional dig-and-replace can take a few days depending on length and access. Trenchless methods are often faster with less yard disruption.

Do I need a permit for this?

Yes. Sewer line replacement requires a permit and inspection under the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the current International Plumbing Code (IPC) with state amendments.

What happens to my landscaping during a traditional replacement?

A trench opens along the line's path. We backfill and do basic restoration, though full landscaping repair like new sod or plantings is typically outside our scope.

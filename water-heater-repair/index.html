Diagnosis

Common Failure Types Explained

Thermocouple Or Igniter Failure

On gas units, a worn thermocouple or igniter is one of the most common reasons a pilot light won't stay lit or the burner won't fire at all. This is usually a straightforward part swap.

Heating Element Failure

On electric units, one or both heating elements can burn out over time, leading to lukewarm water or no hot water at all depending on which element failed.

Failed Thermostat

A thermostat that's reading incorrectly can cause water that's too hot, too cold, or inconsistent, even when the heating element itself is fine.

Sediment Buildup

Mineral sediment settling at the bottom of a tank reduces efficiency, can cause popping or rumbling noises, and accelerates wear on the tank itself over years.

Pressure Relief Valve Issues

A valve that's stuck, leaking, or triggering too often can point to either a failed valve or an excess pressure problem elsewhere in the system.

Anode Rod Depletion

The sacrificial anode rod protects the tank from corrosion. Once it's depleted, the tank itself becomes vulnerable to rust and eventual failure.

Gas Vs. Electric

Repair Differences By Fuel Type

Gas and electric water heaters fail in different ways and call for different diagnostic approaches. Gas units more often have issues with the burner assembly, thermocouple, gas valve, or venting, which require checking for proper flame and combustion in addition to standard water-side diagnostics.

Electric units are more likely to have issues isolated to a specific heating element or thermostat, and diagnosis often starts with checking each element's resistance individually to identify exactly which one has failed, rather than replacing both preemptively.

When To Stop Repairing

When A Repair Isn't The Better Value Anymore

Most water heater calls end in a repair, not a replacement, and that's the right outcome most of the time. A unit reaching the end of its typical 10 to 15 year lifespan that needs a second or third repair within a short window is a different situation. At that point, continuing to repair an aging unit often costs more over time than a single replacement with a new warranty attached.

We'll always tell you honestly which side of that line your specific unit falls on, rather than defaulting toward whichever recommendation costs more.

Water Type

How Local Water Affects Your Unit's Lifespan

Water hardness varies by supplier even within our coverage area, and harder water accelerates sediment buildup and scale accumulation inside a tank. Homes on well water or in areas with higher mineral content tend to need more frequent flushing to keep a unit running efficiently.

If you're seeing repeated sediment-related issues, a water hardness test can confirm whether a water softener would meaningfully extend your unit's service life going forward, rather than just treating each sediment flush as an isolated fix.

Warranty

How Repairs Interact With Your Warranty

Most water heaters carry a manufacturer's warranty on the tank itself, separate from any warranty on parts or labor for a repair. If your unit is still within its original tank warranty, it's worth checking before paying for a repair, since some manufacturers cover specific tank defects directly.

We'll note your unit's make, model, and approximate age during a repair visit, and can help you check whether a specific issue might be covered before you pay out of pocket for something the manufacturer should be handling.

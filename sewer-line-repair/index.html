Signs Of A Sewer Line Problem

What Brings Alpharetta Homeowners To This Page

Recurring Backups

A drain that clogs again soon after clearing often points to a line problem, not a simple clog.

Sewage Odor In The Yard Or Home

Can indicate a break or crack allowing gas or waste to escape underground.

Soggy Spots In The Yard

A patch of unusually lush or wet grass can mean a leaking sewer line beneath it.

Multiple Drains Backing Up Together

Usually points to the main line rather than an individual fixture.

Before We Arrive

What To Do While You Wait

Avoid Contact With Backed-Up Water

Sewage backup can carry bacteria; keep children and pets away from the affected area until it's cleared.

Don't Use Affected Fixtures

Stop using toilets, sinks, or drains connected to the backup until the line is cleared, to avoid making it worse.

Ventilate If There's A Strong Odor

Open windows or run a fan if you notice a sewage smell, since it can indicate escaping gas as well as backup.

Call Us Before Attempting DIY Clearing

A main line backup usually needs professional cabling or camera work, not a household plunger or store-bought chemical.

Local Factors

What This Looks Like In Alpharetta

Alpharetta's mature tree canopy is a genuine, recurring cause of sewer line damage, since roots exploit joint gaps in older clay and cast-iron pipe.

Homes near Alpharetta's historic core are more likely to have original clay sewer lines, which are more prone to joint separation and root intrusion than the PVC used in newer construction.

A camera inspection is worth doing before committing to a repair method, since it can be the difference between a targeted spot repair and unnecessarily replacing a whole line at much greater cost.

Sewer line problems tend to announce themselves gradually, a slow drain here, an odd smell there, before turning into a full backup, so catching the pattern early usually means a smaller, less disruptive, and less expensive repair.

Homes on larger Alpharetta lots with mature trees planted close to the sewer line's path are the cases we most often recommend a proactive camera check for, even without a current backup or any visible symptom yet at all.

FAQs

Common Questions

How is this different from Sewer Line Replacement?

Repair addresses a specific damaged section found by camera. Full replacement is for a line that's failed extensively or is beyond spot repair across most of its length. See our Sewer Line Replacement page for that particular scope.

Will you need to dig up my yard?

Not always. Spot repair and lining can often address damage with less digging than a full replacement, though the camera inspection determines what's actually possible for your line.

How do you find the problem without digging first?

A camera inspection run through the line locates the damage before any digging starts, so you know the scope before agreeing to any work.

Can tree roots cause this kind of damage?

Yes, root intrusion at aging pipe joints is one of the most common causes we see in Alpharetta's established neighborhoods, especially where clay or cast-iron pipe is still in use rather than modern PVC.

Does the repair follow a specific plumbing code?

Yes. Sewer line repairs are done to the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the current International Plumbing Code with state amendments, and most repairs of this scope require a permit.

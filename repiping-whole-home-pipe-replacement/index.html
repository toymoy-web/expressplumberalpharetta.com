Signs A Home Needs Repiping

When Patch Repairs Aren't Enough

Recurring Pinhole Leaks

Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside; once one section starts failing, nearby sections often aren't far behind.

Polybutylene Pipe

This 1980s-90s material is prone to failure and is a common reason for a full repipe regardless of visible leaks yet.

Low Water Pressure Throughout The House

Internal corrosion narrowing pipe diameter over the whole system, not just one fixture.

Discolored Water From Multiple Fixtures

Rust or corrosion affecting the whole supply system, not an isolated fixture issue.

Local Factors

What This Looks Like In Alpharetta

Homes in Alpharetta built in the 1980s and into the early 1990s are the ones most likely to still have polybutylene pipe, a material widely recognized as prone to failure regardless of visible symptoms yet.

Older sections of Alpharetta near the historic core with original galvanized supply lines are common repipe candidates once patch repairs start happening more than once a year.

A repipe is a significant project, so we walk through the full scope and timeline before quoting, rather than treating it like a standard repair call.

FAQs

Common Questions

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

Most single-family homes take a few days, depending on size and layout.

Will you patch the walls afterward?

Yes, we patch openings to a paintable finish; final paint matching is on the homeowner unless otherwise arranged.

Do I need to move out during the repipe?

Most homeowners stay, though water is off for portions of each day; we coordinate scheduling around your household's needs.

Is PEX or copper better for my home?

Both are code-compliant and durable. PEX costs less and is more forgiving in tight spaces; copper has a longer track record. We'll walk through the tradeoff for your specific home.

Will a repipe pass inspection the first time?

We plan the job to meet current Georgia plumbing code from the outset, and we schedule the required inspection as part of the project timeline rather than treating it as an afterthought.

Tank Vs. Tankless

Which Fits Your Alpharetta Home

Traditional Tank

  • Lower upfront cost.
  • Simpler installation.
  • Limited hot water supply.
  • Roughly 10 to 15 year lifespan.

Tankless

  • Continuous hot water on demand.
  • Typically lower running cost over time.
  • Compact, wall-mounted footprint.
  • Longer typical service life than tank units.
Sizing

Choosing The Right Size And Type

Sizing a replacement correctly matters more than most homeowners expect. A tank that's too small means running out of hot water during normal household use, and a tank that's too large wastes energy keeping water hot you're not using.

Tank Capacity

Sized to household size and peak usage times, typically 40 to 50 gallons for most single-family homes, larger for bigger households.

Tankless Flow Rate

Measured in gallons per minute, sized to how many fixtures might run hot water simultaneously in your home.

Fuel Type

Gas units generally recover faster and cost less to operate where natural gas is available. Electric units cost less upfront and don't require gas line access.

Venting Requirements

Gas units need proper venting, which can affect installation cost and placement options, especially in a tankless conversion.

Conversion

Converting From Tank To Tankless

Switching from a tank to a tankless system is more involved than a straightforward tank-to-tank swap. It often requires upgrading gas line sizing to support the tankless unit's higher demand, adding or modifying venting to meet the unit's specifications, and sometimes electrical work depending on the model.

We scope this honestly during a quote rather than a flat, one-size-fits-all conversion price, since your home's existing gas line, venting, and electrical setup determine how much additional work the conversion actually needs.

Installation Day

What Actually Happens During Installation

Installation starts with disconnecting and removing the old unit, checking that existing connections meet current code before reconnecting to the new unit, and setting the new water heater in place. For a tank unit, this includes connecting the water lines, gas or electrical supply, and the temperature and pressure relief valve.

Once connected, we fill the tank, check for leaks at every connection point, and test proper operation before considering the installation complete. Most standard tank-to-tank replacements are completed in a few hours. Tankless installations and conversions typically take longer given the additional venting and line work involved.

Old Unit Removal

What Happens To Your Old Water Heater

We remove and haul away your old unit as part of a standard installation, so you're not left figuring out disposal yourself or renting a truck to deal with it. Most water heaters are recyclable, and we handle that responsibly rather than sending a working recyclable unit to a landfill unnecessarily.

Timing

How Soon Can You Get A New Unit Installed

Most standard tank replacements can be scheduled same-day or next-day if your existing unit has failed and you're without hot water, since we keep common tank sizes in stock rather than special-ordering for every job. Tankless installations and conversions requiring additional venting or gas line work sometimes need a bit more lead time to source specific parts, but we'll always give you a realistic timeline upfront rather than an optimistic guess.

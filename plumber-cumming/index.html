A Quick History

Cumming's Role As The Forsyth County Seat

Cumming was established in 1834 and named for Colonel William Cumming, a veteran of the War of 1812, shortly after Forsyth County itself was created out of former Cherokee territory in 1832.

The area stayed largely agricultural until Lake Lanier was built in the 1950s, flooding thousands of acres to create a reservoir for Atlanta's water supply and hydroelectric power, and in the process turning the county into a recreation and waterfront destination.

GA-400's extension north through the area in the 1970s accelerated the shift from a small farming county seat to one of the metro Atlanta region's fastest-growing suburban markets, a growth pattern that has continued into the present day.

Local Factors

What Shapes Plumbing Calls In Cumming

Newer Construction Overall

Because so much of the area's growth happened from the 1990s onward, original plumbing materials and ages skew newer here than in older North Fulton cities.

Lake-Adjacent Groundwater

Homes near Lake Lanier and the creeks feeding it can see higher groundwater levels, which is a genuine factor in basement and crawlspace moisture.

Mix Of County Sewer And Septic

Newer subdivisions closer to Cumming's core typically connect to county sewer, while some outlying unincorporated Forsyth properties still rely on septic systems.

Well Water In Some Unincorporated Areas

Properties further from the city center sometimes rely on private wells rather than county water service.

Because Forsyth County's growth has been so recent and so fast, we rarely run into the decades-old galvanized pipe or cast iron issues that come up more often in Alpharetta's historic core. Most Cumming-area calls are fixture-level repairs or new-construction punch list items rather than aging-infrastructure problems.

For lake-adjacent properties, we check for sump pump function and basement moisture as a matter of course, since groundwater exposure is a real, recurring factor in that part of our coverage area.

What to Do Before Help Arrives: A Burst Pipe Checklist

Written by the Alpharetta Express Plumbing team · Emergencies · Published 2026.

A burst pipe is one of the few plumbing problems where the first few minutes genuinely matter. Water can spread into flooring, walls, and belongings faster than most people expect, and a clear, calm response can meaningfully limit the damage before a technician ever arrives. This is a practical, step-by-step version of what we'd tell you over the phone if you called us right now.

Step One: Shut Off The Water

Every home has a main water shutoff valve, usually located where the water line enters the house, in a basement, crawlspace, garage, or utility closet. Turning this off stops water from continuing to flow to the burst section, which is the single most effective thing you can do in the first minute. If you don't already know where yours is, this is worth finding on a calm day, not for the first time during an emergency.

If you can't locate the main shutoff quickly, and there's a shutoff valve specific to the affected fixture or line, that can work as a faster temporary measure. Either way, the goal is the same: stop new water from entering the space.

Step Two: Address Electrical Safety

Water and electricity are a dangerous combination. If water is pooling near outlets, appliances, or anything plugged in, don't touch those outlets or appliances directly. Instead, if it's safe to reach your electrical panel without walking through standing water, switch off power to the affected area at the breaker. If reaching the panel means walking through water, don't risk it. Wait for a professional instead.

A Note On Gas Near The Leak

If the burst pipe is anywhere near a gas water heater or gas line, and you smell gas in addition to seeing water, treat that as a separate and more urgent hazard. Leave the area, don't operate any light switches or electrical devices nearby, and call your gas utility or 911 from a safe location before addressing the water issue.

A gas smell always takes priority over water damage, no matter how much water is actively spreading through the house. Water can be cleaned up. A gas leak in an occupied home cannot wait, and no repair is worth risking your safety over.

Step Three: Move What You Can

Once the water is shut off and electrical safety is addressed, move furniture, electronics, rugs, and anything else you can safely lift out of the affected area. This limits secondary damage while you wait, and it's much easier to do before water has fully soaked into fabric or wood.

Step Four: Document What You See

  • Take a few photos of the burst pipe area and any visible damage before cleanup starts.
  • Note the approximate time you first noticed the issue.
  • Keep any damaged items aside rather than discarding them immediately, in case documentation matters for an insurance claim.

This step takes only a minute or two but can matter later, especially if you end up filing a homeowner's insurance claim. It's much easier to document damage before cleanup begins than to reconstruct it afterward from memory. A phone camera is more than sufficient. You don't need special equipment for this step, and a few clear photos are usually all an adjuster needs to get started.

Step Five: Call For Help

Once the immediate safety steps are handled, call an emergency plumbing line. A burst pipe is exactly the kind of call that should go to a 24/7 emergency number rather than waiting for regular business hours, regardless of what time it happens. Describe what you're seeing as clearly as you can: where the water is coming from, how much has accumulated, and whether you've already shut off the main valve.

What Happens When A Technician Arrives

A technician will confirm the water is fully shut off, assess the extent of the damage, and complete the pipe repair, usually the same visit for a straightforward break. Repairs are done to the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the current International Plumbing Code (IPC) with state amendments. If there's already standing water or soaked flooring by the time help arrives, that cleanup and drying work is typically handled separately from the pipe repair itself, since it requires different equipment and expertise.

Why Pipes Burst In The First Place

In this region, sudden cold snaps catching exposed or under-insulated pipe off guard, aging galvanized supply lines corroding from the inside, and ground movement from the area's clay-heavy soil are all real, common causes. We see this pattern often enough in older Sandy Springs homes that it's rarely a total surprise. Knowing the cause doesn't change what to do in the moment, but it can help you and a technician have a useful conversation about whether it's a one-time event or a sign of a broader issue worth addressing.

After The Repair: Reducing The Chance Of A Repeat

Once the immediate repair is done, it's worth asking your technician whether the burst was an isolated event or a symptom of something broader, like widespread aging galvanized pipe that's likely to fail again elsewhere. If a cold snap was the trigger, adding insulation to exposed pipe runs in unheated spaces like a garage or crawlspace is a straightforward, inexpensive step that reduces the odds of a repeat during the next cold spell.

If the burst happened because of consistently high water pressure rather than temperature, a pressure-reducing valve can address that underlying cause rather than just fixing the immediate break. This is the kind of follow-up work that falls under general plumbing repair rather than the emergency call itself. A technician can measure your home's static water pressure during the repair visit and let you know if it's running higher than the typical residential range.

Insurance And Documentation Afterward

Most homeowner's insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe, though coverage details vary by policy, and gradual damage from a long-ignored leak is sometimes treated differently than a sudden break. The photos and notes you took in step four are useful here, along with any invoice or documentation from the plumber who completed the repair. If cleanup and drying were needed beyond the pipe repair itself, that documentation is typically handled by whoever performed the water damage restoration work.

Common Questions

What if I don't know where my main water shutoff valve is?

Look near where the water line enters your home: a basement, crawlspace, utility closet, or garage are common locations. Finding it now, before an emergency, is worth the few minutes it takes.

Is it safe to use a wet vac on standing water myself?

If the water isn't near any electrical outlets or plugged-in equipment, a wet vac can help in the short term. If there's any question about electrical safety, wait for a professional instead.

Should I call my insurance company before or after the plumber?

Either order can work, but don't delay calling the plumber while water is actively flowing. You can call your insurance company once the immediate emergency is under control.

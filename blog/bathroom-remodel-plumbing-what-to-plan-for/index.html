Planning a Bathroom Remodel? Here's the Plumbing Side of It

Written by the Alpharetta Express Plumbing team · Home Maintenance · Published 2026.

Bathroom remodels tend to focus on tile, fixtures, and finishes, which is understandable since that's what you'll see every day. The plumbing behind the walls is easy to overlook during planning, but it's often the part that actually determines your timeline and budget once work begins.

The Most Common Plumbing-Related Decision: Moving Fixtures

Keeping your toilet, sink, and shower in their existing locations keeps plumbing work minimal, since the existing supply and drain lines are already in the right place. Moving any of them, even a couple of feet, means rerouting pipe, which adds real cost and time compared to a layout that keeps everything where it already is.

Tub-To-Shower Conversions

This is one of the more common remodel requests we see, and it involves more plumbing work than people initially expect. Removing a tub for a walk-in shower usually means relocating the drain to the shower's new footprint and installing a new valve, which is shower and bathtub installation work that should be scoped before demo starts, not discovered partway through.

Faucet And Fixture Upgrades

Updating faucets and sink fixtures is comparatively simple if you're keeping the same sink location, and it's a good opportunity to also check the condition of supply lines and shutoff valves behind the wall while everything is already open. See our faucet and sink repair page for what this typically involves on its own.

What Usually Gets Discovered Mid-Remodel

  • Older supply lines behind a wall that turn out to need replacement anyway.
  • A drain location that doesn't quite match new fixture placement without adjustment.
  • Water damage from a long-standing minor leak that wasn't visible until walls opened up.

None of these are unusual, but they're exactly why a remodel's plumbing scope should include some flexibility for what's found once the walls are actually open.

Permits And Code Requirements

Plumbing work involving drain relocation or new rough-in requires a permit and inspection under the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the current International Plumbing Code with state amendments. This isn't optional for a legitimate remodel, and skipping it can complicate a future home sale if the work isn't documented as code-compliant.

Coordinating With Your Other Contractors

A bathroom remodel usually involves a general contractor, a tile installer, and a plumber, often in a specific sequence. We coordinate our part of the timeline with your other contractors rather than working in isolation, since plumbing rough-in typically needs to happen before tile and finish work can proceed.

A Realistic Timeline

The plumbing portion of a typical remodel, assuming no major fixture relocation, is usually completed in a day or two. Larger projects involving moved fixtures or full rough-in for an addition take longer and should be scoped individually rather than estimated from a general rule of thumb.

Venting Considerations Often Missed

Every plumbing fixture needs proper venting to work correctly and to meet code, and moving a fixture sometimes means the existing vent no longer lines up with its new location. This is one of the less visible but genuinely important pieces of a remodel's plumbing scope, and it's worth confirming early rather than discovering a venting problem after tile work is already done.

Water Heater Capacity For A Larger Bathroom

If your remodel adds a larger soaking tub or a multi-head shower, it's worth checking whether your existing water heater can actually keep up with the increased demand. This is a good moment to evaluate whether a water heater upgrade makes sense alongside the remodel, rather than discovering a capacity issue after everything is finished.

Budgeting For The Unexpected

Even a well-planned remodel budget should have some room for what gets discovered once walls are open. This isn't us padding an estimate. It reflects genuine uncertainty about what's behind a wall until it's actually opened, especially in an older home where the original plumbing installation may not be fully documented.

A Note On DIY Demo Before We Arrive

If your contractor or you plan to handle demo before our plumbing work starts, confirm with us which walls are safe to open and which contain supply or drain lines you'd rather have a plumber handle directly. This avoids accidentally damaging a line during demo that then complicates the plumbing scope unnecessarily.

Accessibility Upgrades Worth Considering

If you're already opening up a bathroom for a remodel, it's a reasonable time to consider accessibility features like a curbless shower entry or grab bar blocking behind the wall, even if you don't need them immediately. Adding blocking during construction is far simpler than retrofitting it into finished walls later if your needs change down the road.

This is a common enough request in Alpharetta's more established neighborhoods, where homeowners planning to stay in their homes long-term want to plan ahead rather than remodel twice.

Final Walkthrough Before Tile Goes Down

Before any tile or finish work begins, it's worth doing a final walkthrough of the rough plumbing with your contractor and plumber together, confirming fixture placement one more time while changes are still easy and inexpensive to make. Once tile is set, moving anything becomes a much bigger, costlier undertaking.

Working Around An Occupied Home

If this is your only bathroom, or one of very few, it's worth discussing a plan for water access during the plumbing portion of the work. We can often phase the work to keep at least partial access available, or coordinate timing so the disruption is as short as possible for your household.

A Final Word On Timing Your Project

Scheduling a remodel during a less busy season, rather than right before a holiday or a planned event at your home, gives everyone involved more flexibility if something takes longer than expected. This is a small planning detail that reduces stress considerably if the unexpected does come up.

We're happy to work around your preferred timeline once we understand the full scope of the project.

The earlier we're brought into the planning conversation, the more accurately we can help you avoid surprises once demo actually starts.

Common Questions

Does moving my toilet really add that much to the cost?

Yes, relocating any fixture means rerouting supply and drain lines, which is real additional labor compared to keeping fixtures in their existing footprint.

Can you just do the plumbing rough-in and let my contractor handle the rest?

Yes, we regularly work as part of a larger remodel team and can scope just the plumbing portion.

What if you find a problem behind the wall we didn't know about?

We'll stop and discuss it with you before continuing, rather than including surprise costs without your approval first.

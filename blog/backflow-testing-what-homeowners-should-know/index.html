Backflow Testing: What Homeowners With Irrigation Should Know

Written by the Alpharetta Express Plumbing team · Home Maintenance · Published 2026.

If your home has an in-ground irrigation system, there's a good chance you have a backflow preventer installed, and an even better chance you've never thought about it since the day it was installed. Annual testing is a real requirement in many cases, not an optional extra.

What Backflow Prevention Actually Does

A backflow preventer stops water from flowing in reverse from your irrigation system, or another non-potable source, back into the clean drinking water supply. Without it, fertilizer, pesticides, or standing water from your yard could theoretically be pulled back into the pipes that supply your household's drinking water under certain pressure conditions.

Why Testing Is Required, Not Just Recommended

Many municipalities and water providers require annual backflow testing specifically because the consequence of a failed device is contamination of the shared water supply, not just an inconvenience to one household. This is a public health safeguard, which is why cross-connection control is addressed directly in the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code rather than left as a suggestion.

What Happens During A Test

  • A certified technician connects testing equipment to the device.
  • The device is checked for proper function under test conditions.
  • Results are documented for your records or a required compliance submission.
  • If it fails, we quote a repair or replacement before doing any work.

Most backflow testing visits are quick, especially for a straightforward residential irrigation setup. The device itself is usually a repair rather than a full replacement if it fails, which keeps most of these visits inexpensive relative to what people sometimes expect.

What Happens If You Skip It

Beyond the compliance issue, an untested device that's actually failed provides no real protection, which defeats the purpose of having one installed in the first place. Some water providers will flag a property for non-compliance, which can complicate things at an inconvenient time, like during a home sale.

New Irrigation Installations

If you're having an irrigation system installed for the first time, a backflow preventer is typically required as part of that installation if one isn't already in place. This is worth confirming with your irrigation contractor before the work is finished, not after.

Commercial Properties Have Their Own Requirements

Businesses, especially those with larger irrigation systems or specific water uses, often have more frequent testing requirements than residential customers. If you manage a commercial property, this is worth confirming as part of broader commercial plumbing maintenance rather than treating it as a separate, easily forgotten task.

A Practical Reminder System

For repeat customers, we keep a record of testing dates so we can reach out before the next annual requirement comes due, rather than leaving it entirely up to you to track on your own calendar.

How To Tell If You Already Have A Device Installed

Look for a visible assembly near where your irrigation line branches off from the main water supply, often above ground near the house or at the edge of the yard. It typically has two shutoff valves and test cocks visible on the housing. If you're not sure, we can identify it during a visit rather than you needing to search on your own.

What A Failed Device Actually Means

A failed test doesn't necessarily mean contamination has occurred. It means the device isn't reliably providing the protection it's designed for, which is a real risk worth addressing rather than ignoring until it becomes an actual problem. Repair is usually possible without full replacement, keeping the cost manageable for most residential situations.

Documentation You Should Keep

Hold onto your annual test certificate. Many municipalities and some insurers require proof of current testing, and having it readily available saves you from a scramble if it's ever requested, whether by your water provider or during a home sale.

A Regional Note On Requirements

Testing requirements can vary by water provider even within the same general metro area, so what applies to a property in one part of town isn't automatically identical everywhere. We can help confirm your specific requirement rather than you needing to research your provider's exact policy yourself.

Combining Testing With Other Seasonal Maintenance

Many homeowners schedule backflow testing alongside spring irrigation startup, since the system is already being checked over for the season ahead. This is a practical way to handle both tasks in one visit rather than treating them as entirely separate calls throughout the year.

If your irrigation contractor doesn't handle backflow testing themselves, we can coordinate directly with them so nothing falls through the gap between two different service providers working on the same system.

What We Check Beyond The Device Itself

During a testing visit, we also take a quick look at the surrounding plumbing and connections, since a device can test fine while nearby fittings show early signs of wear. Catching that during a routine test is more convenient than a separate call later for something that could have been noted at the same visit.

This kind of small additional check doesn't add meaningfully to the visit's time, but it's the difference between treating testing as a checkbox exercise and actually using the visit to catch problems early.

What If You're Selling Your Home

A current backflow test certificate is sometimes requested during a home sale, particularly if the property has an irrigation system. Having recent, documented testing on file can smooth this part of the closing process rather than becoming a last-minute scramble to schedule testing before a deadline.

If you're not sure whether your paperwork is current or complete, we can pull our own records for your address and help fill in the gap before it becomes an issue during a sale.

This kind of small proactive step tends to save real time and hassle later, especially during a transaction with its own deadlines to manage.

Feel free to call ahead of a planned sale so we can get everything in order well before it's actually needed.

A quick phone call is usually all it takes to get scheduled, whether it's your first test or a routine annual renewal.

We're here to help either way.

What To Do If You Suspect Contamination

  • Stop using tap water for drinking or cooking until the situation is checked.
  • Note any unusual taste, color, or odor you noticed and when.
  • Contact your water utility to report the concern.
  • Call us to inspect the backflow device itself for a possible failure.

This is a rare situation, not a common one, but it's worth knowing the basic steps rather than guessing if it ever comes up.

Common Questions

Do I actually need this if I just have a small irrigation system?

Many municipalities require it regardless of system size, since the risk it addresses isn't proportional to how large your irrigation system is. We can help confirm your specific requirement.

What happens if my backflow preventer fails the test?

We'll quote a repair or replacement, then retest to confirm it passes before certifying it.

How long does a typical residential test take?

Most straightforward residential tests are completed in well under an hour.

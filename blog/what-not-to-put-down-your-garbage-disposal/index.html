What Can (And Can't) Go Down Your Garbage Disposal

Written by the Alpharetta Express Plumbing team · Fixtures · Published 2026.

A garbage disposal feels like it should handle anything that fits down the drain, but a handful of common kitchen scraps are behind most of the jam and clog calls we get. Knowing what to avoid extends the life of the unit and saves you a repair call down the line.

What Handles Fine

  • Small food scraps like vegetable trimmings and soft fruit.
  • Ice cubes, which actually help clean the blades.
  • Small bones from soft-boned fish, in moderation.
  • Citrus peels, in small amounts, which can help with odor.

What Causes The Most Jams And Clogs

  • Grease and cooking oil, which solidify inside pipes and coat the disposal's components.
  • Fibrous vegetables like celery, corn husks, and onion skins, which wrap around the blades instead of chopping cleanly.
  • Starchy foods like pasta and rice, which expand with water and can clog downstream pipes.
  • Coffee grounds, which accumulate into a dense sludge over time rather than washing through.
  • Eggshells, which are debated but often contribute to sediment buildup around the blades.

None of these will destroy a disposal in one use, but repeated exposure is exactly the pattern behind a lot of the garbage disposal repair calls we get.

What A Jam Actually Feels Like

A jammed disposal typically hums without spinning, which usually means something is physically stuck against the blades rather than a motor failure. Most jams like this are clearable safely from outside the unit using the reset button and the hex key that comes with most disposals, without needing to reach inside near the blades.

When It's Actually A Drain Problem, Not A Disposal Problem

If the disposal runs fine but water backs up into the sink, that's more likely a downstream clog than a disposal issue itself. This is a common enough distinction that it's worth mentioning when you call, since it points toward drain cleaning rather than a disposal repair.

How Long Should A Disposal Actually Last

Typically 8 to 12 years with normal household use, though what actually goes down the drain regularly affects that range more than almost anything else. A household that's careful about grease and fibrous scraps often gets more years out of a unit than one that treats the disposal as a catch-all.

A Simple Maintenance Habit

Running cold water for a few seconds before and after using the disposal helps flush waste through rather than letting it sit in the pipe. This small habit, combined with avoiding the items above, is most of what keeps a disposal running well for years without a repair call.

Pasta And Rice Deserve A Special Mention

These seem harmless since they're soft and easy to chop, but they continue absorbing water and expanding well after they've passed through the disposal itself. That expansion happens further down the pipe, where it contributes to clogs that have nothing to do with the disposal's blades or motor.

Grease Is The Single Biggest Culprit

Cooking oil and grease seem liquid enough to pour down the drain safely, but they solidify as they cool inside the pipe, gradually narrowing it from the inside. Over time, this buildup is one of the most common reasons a kitchen line needs professional cleaning rather than a quick fix. Pouring grease into a separate container for disposal, rather than down the sink, is a simple habit that prevents a lot of future problems.

When A Repair Isn't Worth It Anymore

A disposal that's original to a home built in the 1990s or early 2000s is well past typical service life. A repair call on a unit that age often turns into a replacement conversation once we're actually looking at it, since patching an old unit rarely makes sense compared to installing a new one with a full warranty.

Choosing A Replacement Unit

Higher horsepower models handle tougher food waste with less jamming, and quieter models have become more common as a standard feature rather than a premium upgrade. We can walk through options that fit your household's cooking habits and budget during an installation visit.

A Word On Chemical Drain Cleaners Near A Disposal

Pouring a chemical drain cleaner into a disposal to address a clog or smell isn't a great idea. These products can damage the disposal's internal components over repeated use, and they don't address whatever's actually causing the smell or slow drainage in the first place. A proper flush with ice, citrus, and cold water is a safer routine maintenance habit.

If a smell or slow drain persists after basic cleaning, that's worth a real diagnostic visit rather than continuing to treat the symptom with another round of chemicals.

Setting Expectations For A New Household

If you've recently moved into a home with an existing disposal, it's worth a quick conversation with anyone in the household about what actually goes down it, since habits from a previous kitchen don't always transfer cleanly. A few minutes spent on this upfront can meaningfully extend the life of a unit you didn't originally install yourself.

What To Do If Something Wrong Already Went Down

Say you've already put something down the disposal that shouldn't have gone in, like a large amount of grease or a starchy food. Running plenty of cold water afterward and avoiding hot water for that drain in the short term can help limit how much solidifies inside the pipe. If a clog develops anyway, that's a job for standard drain cleaning rather than repeated disposal cycling, which won't clear a downstream blockage.

It's a small precaution that can save you an unnecessary call.

And if you're ever unsure whether something is safe to put down the disposal, it's worth asking rather than guessing.

A quick question now is always cheaper than an avoidable repair call later on.

Common Questions

Is it true I shouldn't put eggshells down the disposal?

It's debated among plumbers. They won't damage the blades, but the membrane and shell fragments can contribute to buildup over time, so many technicians recommend composting them instead.

My disposal smells bad even though it's working fine, what should I do?

Try running ice cubes and a bit of citrus peel through it, and flush with cold water afterward. If the smell persists, it may be a drain-side issue rather than the disposal itself.

Can I install a garbage disposal where there wasn't one before?

Yes, as long as your sink and under-cabinet plumbing and wiring can accommodate it. We can check that during a visit and quote accordingly.

Slab Leaks: The Warning Signs Homeowners Often Miss

Written by the Alpharetta Express Plumbing team · Leak Detection · Published 2026.

A slab leak happens under your home's concrete foundation, out of sight and usually out of mind until it's caused real damage. Unlike a visible pipe burst, the early signs are quiet, and homeowners often miss them for months before something more obvious forces the issue.

Why Slab Leaks Are Different

Most plumbing leaks show themselves fairly quickly: a wet spot, a drip, a stain. A slab leak is buried under a layer of concrete, sometimes under flooring on top of that, so water has to travel somewhere before it becomes visible. By the time it does, the leak has often been running for a while.

Signs Worth Paying Attention To

  • A warm spot on the floor, especially over a hot water line, that has no other explanation.
  • The sound of running water audible through the floor when every fixture in the house is off.
  • An unexplained increase in your water bill with no matching change in household use.
  • A damp or musty smell in a room with no visible moisture source.
  • Cracking or slight unevenness in flooring, which in advanced cases can follow sustained moisture under the slab.

Any single one of these could have another explanation. Together, or persisting over time, they're worth having checked rather than dismissed.

How A Slab Leak Is Actually Found

Slab leaks call for different equipment than a typical wall or ceiling leak. We use acoustic listening equipment and thermal imaging to trace the leak's position before anything gets opened. This matters because breaking concrete in the wrong spot means paying for repair and patching twice instead of once.

Once slab leak detection narrows the location, there are usually two paths: opening the specific section of slab needed to reach the pipe, or rerouting the line above the slab entirely to avoid breaking concrete at all. Which one makes sense depends on the leak's location and your home's layout.

Why Catching It Early Actually Matters

A slab leak caught from a warm floor spot or an odd water bill is a smaller, more contained problem than one that's been running long enough to affect flooring or cause visible water damage. The financial difference between the two isn't small. Early detection is genuinely one of the more cost-effective calls a homeowner can make.

This Is Related To, But Different From, General Leak Detection

If a leak is behind a wall or in the ceiling rather than under the slab, that's a different situation entirely. That's a job for standard leak detection, which uses similar tracing principles but a different diagnostic approach suited to accessible plumbing rather than buried foundation lines.

A Local Factor Worth Knowing

Alpharetta's dense red clay soil shifts with moisture and can put ongoing stress on a slab and the plumbing beneath it over the years. Homes built on slab foundations became especially common in newer subdivisions from the 1990s onward, which is part of why slab leak calls skew toward newer construction here rather than the pier-and-beam homes sometimes found closer to the historic core.

We see this pattern often enough in Sandy Springs's newer construction that a warm floor spot or an unexplained bill jump is worth calling about rather than waiting to see if it resolves on its own.

How This Differs From A Typical Water Bill Spike

Not every water bill increase points to a slab leak. A running toilet, an irrigation system left on too long, or a forgotten hose can all explain a jump just as easily. What makes a slab leak worth suspecting specifically is a bill increase with no explanation you can find anywhere else in the house, combined with one of the other signs like a warm floor spot or audible running water.

What Happens During A Typical Detection Visit

  • We ask about what you've noticed and when it started.
  • Acoustic equipment listens for the sound signature of water escaping under pressure.
  • Thermal imaging can spot a warm zone consistent with a hot water line leak.
  • We confirm the location before recommending repair or reroute.

Hot Water Line Leaks Versus Cold Water Line Leaks

A leak in a hot water line under the slab is often easier to detect early, since it creates a noticeable warm spot on the floor above it. A cold water line leak doesn't have that same tell, which is part of why the water bill and the sound of running water are especially important signs to watch for when the leak is on the cold side.

Why We Don't Guess At The Fix

Breaking concrete without confirming the exact location first means a real chance of opening the wrong spot, which costs you a second repair and a second patch instead of one. The tracing step takes extra time upfront, but it's what keeps a slab leak repair from turning into two separate jobs.

When To Call Immediately Versus When To Monitor

A warm floor spot with no other symptoms is worth a call, but it's not usually an emergency in the way a burst pipe is. An unexplained water bill spike combined with visible flooring damage or a musty smell is a stronger signal to call sooner rather than later, since that combination suggests the leak has been running longer.

If you're ever unsure whether what you're seeing warrants an urgent call, describe it to us over the phone and we'll help you figure out how quickly it needs attention.

The Bottom Line

A slab leak is one of the few plumbing issues where the diagnosis genuinely matters as much as the repair. Getting the location right the first time, rather than guessing, is what keeps a slab leak from turning into a bigger, more expensive project than it needed to be.

Newer construction along the GA-400 corridor and older homes closer to the historic core can both develop a slab leak, though the underlying pipe materials and slab age differ enough that the repair approach sometimes looks different from one home to the next.

Common Questions

Will you have to break up my whole floor to find a slab leak?

No. We trace the leak's location first, so only the specific section that needs it, if any, gets opened.

Is rerouting always better than repairing under the slab?

Not always. It depends on the leak's location and your home's layout. We'll walk through both options once the leak is located.

Can a slab leak cause structural problems?

Left unaddressed long enough, yes, it can affect flooring and subfloor. That's part of why catching it early through the warning signs matters.

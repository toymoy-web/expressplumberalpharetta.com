Before We Arrive

What To Do While You Wait

If you have an active leak or burst pipe, a few steps can limit damage before a technician gets there.

Shut Off The Water

Find your main water shutoff valve and close it if it's safe to reach. This stops the flow at the source.

Cut Power Near Standing Water

If water is near outlets or appliances, switch off power to that area at the breaker rather than touching the outlets directly.

Move Valuables Clear

Get furniture, electronics, and rugs away from the wet area to limit secondary damage while you wait.

Document Before Cleanup

A few photos of the leak and any damage help later, whether for us or for an insurance claim.

Local Plumbing Factors

Why Alpharetta Homes Call Us

Alpharetta sits in northern Fulton County, along the GA-400 tech corridor. Growth here took off in the 1990s. Newer subdivisions now sit alongside an older in-town core near the original courthouse square. That mix shapes what actually breaks in a given home.

Georgia Red Clay Soil

The Piedmont region's dense red clay shifts with rainfall. That movement puts steady stress on buried water and sewer lines over years. It's a genuine regional factor, not a sales pitch.

Newer Construction, Older Core

Most Alpharetta housing dates from the 1990s through the 2010s. The downtown core is older. Pipe age and material vary block by block as a result.

Tree Root Intrusion

Mature landscaping across established Alpharetta neighborhoods is common. Root intrusion is a recurring cause of slow drains and sewer line backups in these areas.

Statewide Licensing

Georgia plumbing licenses are issued statewide by the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board (Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers), and plumbing work is governed by the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with state amendments. We can confirm our license status on request before any work begins.

Because Alpharetta covers both eras of construction, we don't assume a single fix works everywhere. A tech-corridor townhome from 2015 and a house near the historic square from the 1970s call for different diagnostics, even for the same symptom.

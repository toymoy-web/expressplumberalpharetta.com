Common Toilet Problems

What We Fix Most Often

Running Toilet

Usually a worn flapper or fill valve, one of the quickest fixes we do.

Weak Or Incomplete Flush

Can be a clogged jet, low water level in the tank, or a worn flush valve.

Rocking Or Leaking At The Base

Often a failed wax ring, which requires pulling and resetting the toilet.

Cracked Tank Or Bowl

A crack means replacement, not repair, regardless of the toilet's age.

Local Factors

What This Looks Like In Alpharetta

Older homes near Alpharetta's historic core sometimes have toilets on older flange heights or bolt patterns, which a technician accounts for when resetting or replacing a unit.

Newer Alpharetta subdivisions built since the 1990s more often already have water-efficient toilets installed, so replacement calls there are more often about a cracked tank or a failed mechanism than an efficiency upgrade.

A toilet that rocks slightly when you sit on it is worth addressing even before it leaks, since a failed wax ring seal can let water reach the subfloor unnoticed.

We carry common wax rings, fill valves, and flappers on the truck for most standard toilets, which is why so many of these calls are finished in a single visit.

If a toilet is the only one in the house, we understand that's not a repair anyone wants to wait on, and we prioritize those calls accordingly whenever our schedule allows it.

FAQs

Common Questions

My toilet runs on and off all day, is that a big deal?

It wastes water and usually points to a worn flapper or fill valve. It's a quick, inexpensive fix, but worth doing since it adds up meaningfully on your water bill over a full billing cycle if it's left alone.

Should I repair or replace an older toilet?

If it's cracked or you're repairing it repeatedly, replacement is usually the better value. A technician can walk you through the tradeoff for your specific unit, its age, and how it's been performing lately.

Do you install low-flow or comfort-height toilets?

Yes, we can install whichever style fits your household best, including water-efficient and accessibility-focused models from major brands.

How long does a full replacement take?

Most toilet replacements are completed in under two hours total, including removing and hauling away the old unit, setting the new wax ring, and testing the new one for leaks before we leave the house.

Signs Of A Pool Leak

What Brings Alpharetta Homeowners To This Page

Water Loss Beyond Normal Evaporation

Losing more than about a quarter inch of water per day, once evaporation is accounted for, usually points to an actual leak.

Wet Spots Near The Equipment Pad

Standing water or soggy ground around the pump, filter, or heater often traces back to a fitting or plumbing leak at the pad.

A Pool That Won't Hold Its Water Level Overnight

A consistent, repeatable drop overnight is a stronger signal than a one-time reading.

Cracks Or Discoloration In The Shell

Visible cracking, especially near steps, lights, or fittings, is a common leak point worth tracing directly.

Local Factors

What This Looks Like In Alpharetta

Alpharetta's larger-lot single-family homes, especially in newer subdivisions along the GA-400 corridor, support a meaningfully higher rate of in-ground pool ownership than many surrounding markets. That's reflected in the number of local pool builders actively working here.

Alpharetta's red clay soil shifts with moisture, and that same movement can stress the plumbing lines running between a pool's equipment pad and the shell over years, similar to how it affects buried water lines elsewhere on a property.

We coordinate directly with pool contractors when a leak turns out to be shell or liner related, so you're not left managing two separate, uncoordinated repair calls for the same underlying problem.

Worth Knowing Before You Call

If a pool leak has gone unnoticed long enough to affect nearby landscaping or hardscaping, that's closer to general leak detection territory for the surrounding property, not just the pool itself.

A fitting or valve repair at the equipment pad sometimes overlaps with general plumbing repair if it's not strictly pool-specific, and any backflow connection between pool plumbing and the household water supply still falls under the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code.

We see a fair number of these calls in Johns Creek's newer subdivisions, where larger lots and in-ground pools are common.

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if it's a real leak and not just evaporation?

A basic bucket test, comparing water loss inside a bucket on the pool step to the pool's actual water loss over 24 hours, is a simple way to tell the two apart before calling anyone.

Do you repair the pool shell itself?

No, shell and liner repairs are typically a pool contractor's scope. We handle the plumbing side, fittings, buried lines, and equipment pad connections, and can coordinate with your pool company if the leak turns out to be shell-related.

Can a small pool leak actually cause real damage?

Yes, especially if the leak is near the foundation or a buried line runs close to the house. A steady, unnoticed leak over months can contribute to soil movement or, in rare cases, structural concerns worth catching early.

Will you need to drain the pool to find the leak?

Not always. Electronic tracing can often locate a leak without draining. Some repairs do require a partial drain to access the specific area, which we'll explain before starting.

Common Causes

Why Pipes Actually Burst

Freezing Temperatures

Water expands as it freezes, and enough pressure buildup inside a pipe can cause it to crack or split, especially in exposed or under-insulated sections.

Aging Galvanized Pipe

Older galvanized supply lines corrode from the inside over decades, gradually weakening until a section finally gives way.

High Water Pressure

Consistently high pressure stresses pipes and fittings over time, making a burst more likely even without an obvious trigger.

Physical Damage

Nail punctures, settling foundations, or accidental impact during other work can compromise a pipe section that then fails later.

Repair Approach

How We Actually Fix It

Once the water is shut off and the area assessed, the repair approach depends on the extent of damage. A single, clean break in an accessible section is often a straightforward cut-and-couple repair, replacing just the damaged segment.

If the burst reveals broader corrosion or damage extending beyond the immediate break, that's a sign the surrounding pipe may need replacement too, not just the failure point itself. We'll show you what we're seeing and explain why a repair might need to extend further than the initial break before we proceed.

After The Fix

What We Check Before Calling The Job Done

A completed repair isn't just stopping the immediate leak. We pressure test the repaired section to confirm it holds under normal operating pressure, and we check nearby fittings and connections for related stress or wear. If the cause was high water pressure, we'll measure your home's static pressure and discuss whether a pressure-reducing valve makes sense to prevent a repeat.

Winter Prevention

Reducing Your Risk Before The Next Cold Snap

If a cold snap was behind your burst pipe, a few preventive steps reduce the odds of a repeat. Insulating exposed pipe runs in unheated spaces like a garage or crawlspace is a straightforward, inexpensive step. Letting a faucet drip slightly during extreme cold keeps water moving through vulnerable sections, reducing freeze risk.

None of this guarantees another pipe won't fail during a severe enough cold event, but it meaningfully reduces the odds for the specific sections most exposed to temperature swings.

Multiple Bursts

What It Means If More Than One Pipe Fails

If you've had more than one burst pipe within a relatively short window, that's usually not a coincidence. It typically signals a broader issue with your home's plumbing, whether that's aging galvanized pipe throughout the system or consistently high water pressure stressing multiple sections at once.

In that situation, we'll talk through whether continuing to repair individual bursts as they happen makes sense, or whether a broader look at your plumbing, potentially including repiping, is worth considering instead.

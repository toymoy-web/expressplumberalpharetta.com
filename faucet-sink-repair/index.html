Common Calls

What We Fix Most Often

Dripping Faucet

Almost always a worn washer, cartridge, or O-ring, not a sign the whole faucet needs replacing.

Low Water Pressure At One Fixture

Often a clogged aerator or a supply line issue specific to that fixture.

Sink Not Draining Properly

Can be a P-trap clog or a partially closed stopper mechanism.

Leaking Under The Sink

Usually a loose or worn supply line connection, caught early before it causes water damage.

Before We Arrive

What To Do While You Wait

Shut Off The Fixture's Valve First

If you're waiting on us and the leak is active, close the small shutoff valve under the sink. That's usually better than the main house valve, since it leaves water on for the rest of the home.

Keep Electrical Items Away From The Leak

Move any plugged-in items away from water pooling under a sink, and avoid touching outlets in a wet cabinet.

Don't Force A Stuck Shutoff Valve

An old valve that won't turn can snap; if it won't move with light pressure, leave it and call us rather than forcing it.

Local Factors

What This Looks Like In Alpharetta

Alpharetta's newer subdivisions typically have PEX supply lines under sinks, which are more flexible to work around than the rigid copper still found in older homes near the historic core.

A slow drip in a home with municipal water is a small but steady cost over a year; even a low-volume leak adds up meaningfully on a water bill.

We see a fair number of under-sink supply line leaks that started small and went unnoticed until a cabinet floor showed water damage, which is why we check those connections during a standard faucet call.

Bathroom faucets tend to fail from mineral deposits inside the cartridge, while kitchen faucets more often wear out from frequency of use, which is part of why the two aren't always fixed with the same part.

If you're renovating a kitchen or bathroom, it's worth having us look at the existing supply lines and shutoff valves at the same time, since matching a new faucet to old, worn valves can cause problems down the line before long.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is a dripping faucet worth fixing right away?

Yes, a slow drip adds up on your water bill over time, and it's usually an inexpensive fix compared to letting it continue for months.

Can you match my existing faucet finish if I need a replacement?

We can source a wide range of finishes and styles; bring us the brand or model if you have a preference, or we can recommend a comparable option.

Why is only one sink low on pressure?

That usually points to something local to that fixture, like a clogged aerator or a partially closed supply valve, rather than a whole-house pressure issue.

Do you check under-sink plumbing during a faucet repair?

Yes, we look at supply lines and the P-trap while we're already there, since that's often where a secondary leak shows up before anyone notices it, and it costs nothing extra to check while we're already under the sink.

Signs Of A Water Line Problem

What Brings Alpharetta Homeowners To This Page

Low Pressure Throughout The House

If every fixture is affected, the issue is more likely the service line than an indoor pipe.

Wet Spot Between The Meter And House

A visible soggy patch along that path is a common sign of a leaking service line.

Unexplained High Water Bill

A leak underground before the water ever reaches the house can run up usage with no indoor sign.

Discolored Water After Ground Work Nearby

Construction or utility work near the line can disturb or damage it.

Local Factors

What This Looks Like In Alpharetta

Alpharetta's red clay soil shifts with moisture and puts ongoing stress on buried service lines over the years, which is a genuine contributing factor to line failures here.

Homes near Alpharetta's historic core sometimes still have older galvanized or, in rare cases, lead-era service lines, both of which are common full-replacement candidates when they fail.

Newer Alpharetta subdivisions typically have PEX or copper service lines, which hold up better against soil movement than the older materials found in some original-era homes.

A service line issue is easy to mistake for an indoor plumbing problem at first, since both can cause low pressure; confirming which one it actually is saves you from paying for the wrong repair.

FAQs

Common Questions

How is this different from a leak inside my house?

Indoor leaks are handled through our Leak Detection or General Plumbing Repair pages. This page covers the buried line between the meter or curb stop and your home specifically.

Will you need to dig up my whole yard?

Not necessarily. Trenchless methods can often replace or repair a line with a couple of small access points instead of a full trench.

Who's responsible for the line, me or the utility?

Typically the homeowner is responsible for the line from the meter to the house; the utility maintains the main up to the meter. We can help clarify this for your specific situation.

How do you find a leak in a buried line?

Pressure testing and leak-tracing equipment narrow the location before any digging starts.

Do you coordinate with the utility if work is needed near the meter?

Yes, we handle that coordination directly so you don't have to manage separate scheduling with the utility yourself.

Is this work permitted under Georgia code?

Yes, water line repair and replacement follows the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the current International Plumbing Code (IPC) with state amendments, and permitting depends on the scope of the work.

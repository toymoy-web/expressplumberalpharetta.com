Warning Signs

What Brings Alpharetta Homeowners To This Page

Pump Running Constantly

A pump that never shuts off usually means a stuck float switch or a pump that's undersized for how much water is actually coming in.

Pump Not Turning On During Rain

If the pit is filling but the pump isn't activating, that's often a float switch or power issue, not a pump that's actually failed.

Loud Grinding Or Rattling

Unusual noise during operation often signals a failing motor or debris caught in the impeller.

Water Still Pooling Despite A Working Pump

This can mean the pump is undersized for the actual water volume, or that a secondary issue like a clogged discharge line is limiting how fast it can clear the pit.

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if my sump pump actually needs repair?

Constant running, failure to activate during rain, unusual noise, or water pooling despite a working pump are all signs worth having checked.

Do I need a battery backup if I already have a working pump?

Not necessarily, but if your area loses power during storms, a backup system protects against exactly the scenario a sump pump exists to prevent.

How long does a sump pump typically last?

Most pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use. A pump nearing that age that's starting to act up is often worth replacing rather than repeatedly repairing.

Can you install a sump pump where there wasn't one before?

Yes, if your crawlspace or basement has a water intrusion issue, we can assess whether a new sump pump system is the right fix and install one.
